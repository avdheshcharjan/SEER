# Designing **Tomo**: Swipe-Based Micro Prediction App on Base

# PHASE 1: MVP
Building **Tomo** involves combining an addictive swipe UX with a robust prediction market backend. Below we break down key design decisions and recommendations for your MVP, including swipe interaction flow, choosing a betting model (pari-mutuel vs. share-based), smart contract integration with gasless transactions, and essential social features like leaderboards and sharing.

## Swipe Interaction & Transaction Batching

To keep the user experience seamless and “mindless,” **batching swipes into fewer transactions** is crucial. You want users to rapidly swipe Yes/No on a stack of prediction cards (similar to Tinder) without seeing a blockchain prompt each time. The solution implemented in your hackathon project already uses **automatic transaction batching**[\[1\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/README.md#L63-L67):

- **Batch by Count:** In your code, once a batch reaches a certain size (e.g. 20 swipes), it auto-triggers a transaction[\[2\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L210-L218). This prevents infinite swiping without ever sending a transaction. The hackathon code used a max batch size of 20, meaning the 21st swipe would cause the first 20 predictions to be sent to the blockchain.
- **Batch by Time:** You also set a timer to auto-execute after a period of inactivity. Originally this was a short window (about 10 seconds of no new swipes)[\[3\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L226-L234). If the user pauses swiping, the pending bets batch is submitted automatically. This ensures bets don’t remain uncommitted if the user stops or leaves the app.

**Recommendation:** Continue using **both a count threshold and a time window** to batch swipes. This “both” approach aligns with your preference and addresses your concern about users swiping endlessly without committing: - For example, you might **increase the idle timer** to allow a longer swiping spree (say 30–60 seconds) if continuous swiping is expected. A longer timer (e.g. 30s) lets engaged users swipe through many cards, making the experience feel continuous and addictive. However, be cautious with too long a timer (like a full 60s) – if a user swipes and then closes the app quickly, a long delay could mean their bets never get submitted. It may be safer to trigger a batch sooner if the app goes to background or after a moderate idle period (15–30s) to ensure bets aren’t lost. - **Batch size:** 20 cards per batch (from your hackathon) is a reasonable default[\[2\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L210-L218). You could adjust this if needed – e.g. batch after 10 or 15 swipes for quicker feedback, or up to ~50 if you want even larger single transactions. Keep in mind **block gas limits**: batching 50+ predictions in one transaction could approach block gas limits or strain your paymaster. Sticking to ~20 ensures the batched call stays within a safe gas range. - **User feedback during batching:** Your current UI provides feedback like “✅ YES added to batch!” for each swipe and shows a “pending predictions” counter in the corner[\[4\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L62-L70). This is great for letting users know their choices are queued. Continue to display a **small notification or counter** of queued bets, and perhaps a subtle **progress timer** if using a time window (e.g. a fading animation to indicate how much time before auto-submit). This maintains the “illusion of control” – users feel like they’re just swiping, but are still aware something will finalize soon. - **Manual submit option:** It’s helpful to allow the user to manually confirm the batch if they wish. In your hackathon code, a modal appears with a **“Confirm X Predictions”** button once batching triggers[\[5\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L519-L527). This gives an explicit confirmation step (especially if using Coinbase’s OnchainKit UI) and can reassure users that their bets are being placed. Since transactions are gasless for them, this confirmation is just a tap, not a wallet gas prompt. You can keep this or even make it automatic – but retaining a confirmation button can prevent accidental bets if the user wants to review the queued list. It’s a UX decision: **for maximum seamlessness, auto-send the batch** (no extra tap) when conditions meet; or **for clarity, require one tap** on a custom confirm dialog.

By combining these triggers, you achieve a balance: Users can **“mindlessly” swipe through dozens of predictions** in one go, but behind the scenes their swipes are chunked into periodic transactions. This prevents an unbounded queue (which could overwhelm the system or never be finalized) and fits the addictive, rapid interaction model. The hack MVP’s approach of ~20 swipes or ~10s idle is a solid starting point[\[2\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L210-L218)[\[3\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L226-L234) – you can tweak these values with user testing to see what keeps flow best. For instance, you might find a 60-second idle window is too long (bets get delayed), whereas something like 15 seconds idle or every 10 swipes feels more responsive.

**Key point:** Make sure any pending bets are auto-submitted if the user stops playing. This likely means keeping a relatively short timeout if the app is backgrounded or user becomes inactive. You don’t want a scenario where a user swipes 5 cards, then forgets to come back within a minute and those bets never get placed. A compromise is to **trigger the batch on app exit or session end** (if detectable) or simply keep the idle timer reasonably short for safety.

# PHASE 2: BETTING SYSTEM
## Betting System: Pari-Mutuel vs. Prediction Market (AMM Shares)

Your next big decision is choosing the **betting mechanism** under the hood. You’ve built a **“SimplePredictionMarket” contract using an AMM-style share system** (buying/selling outcome shares)[\[6\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L9-L12). Let’s compare this with a simpler **pari-mutuel pool** approach:

- **AMM Share Model (Continuous Prediction Market):** In this model, users purchase “YES” or “NO” shares for a question at the current price, which moves based on the pool of funds on each side. Your contract implements a constant-product style pricing: _“YES price = YES pool / (YES pool + NO pool)”_[\[6\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L9-L12). When a user swipes YES for $1, the contract buyShares(true, 1) calculates how many YES shares they receive according to the formula and adds their USDC to the YES pool[\[7\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L218-L227)[\[8\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L236-L244). If they’re correct at resolution, each share pays out 1 USDC[\[9\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L198-L205). If they’re wrong, their shares are worthless. Users could even sell back shares before market end (sellShares) to lock in profit or cut loss, thanks to the AMM providing liquidity.

**Pros:** This gives a dynamic odds market. The price of YES vs NO fluctuates in real-time as people bet, theoretically reflecting the probability. It also means payouts are **variable** – betting on the underdog yields more shares (and thus more profit if you win), while betting on the favorite yields fewer shares (smaller profit, or even a loss, if you win). It’s a _continuous market_ which is great for advanced use (and allows features like trading out before resolution).

**Cons:** Complexity is higher – both in implementation and user comprehension. For a casual, “brainrot” style app, **users aren’t checking odds or prices**; they’re just swiping yes/no on instinct. If you don’t show real-time odds or “price” feedback (and you indicated this isn’t important for your users), a share-based AMM can lead to confusion or hidden outcomes: - A user might bet $1 on a very likely event (e.g. 90% Yes already). They’ll receive perhaps 0.90 shares. If Yes wins, they claim $0.90 – **winning less than they bet**, effectively a loss despite being correct. This is rational in a market odds sense (they paid a high price for a nearly certain outcome), but a casual user could feel cheated (“I guessed right and still lost some money?”). Without showing odds/probabilities, this outcome will be non-intuitive. - Conversely, if they bet on a long-shot and win, they’d get a big payout (many shares \* 1 USDC each) – exciting, but again they wouldn’t know the potential reward upfront since you’re not showing odds. - Implementation-wise, the AMM model requires maintaining liquidity pools, handling edge cases (your contract seeded a minimal liquidity of 10 USDC on each side to start markets[\[10\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L96-L100)), and allowing resolution and claims. It’s already built and working on testnet, but it’s more than what’s needed if you want _simpler backend logic_.

- **Pari-Mutuel Betting (Fixed Odds Pool):** In a pari-mutuel system, users simply place bets on YES or NO, and all the stakes go into two pools. The odds aren’t fixed until the market closes – the payout is determined after resolution by the proportion of the pool. Essentially, **winners split the losers’ stakes** (often each winner gets their original bet back plus a share of the losing pool proportional to their bet size). For example, if total bets on YES = $100 and NO = $50, and the outcome is YES, the $50 losing pool is distributed among the YES bettors. A user who bet $10 on YES would get their $10 back plus (10/100)_$50 = $5, so $15 total (a 1.5× return). If the majority side wins, returns are low (possibly just a small profit); if the minority side wins, returns are high (since fewer winners split a larger losing pool). Crucially,_ _a correct bet never yields less than the original stake_\* (barring fees or edge cases) – at worst, if almost everyone picked correctly, winners get roughly their stake back plus maybe pennies from losers.

**Pros:** Simpler logic and **straightforward resolution**: - The smart contract just needs to track total YES and NO bets (and possibly each user’s bet for payout calculation). When the market resolves (e.g. you, as the resolver, set the outcome after 24h), you compute payouts based on final pool sizes. You could either distribute funds automatically or let users claim their winnings. No complex math during betting – a bet is just an amount added to one side’s total. - Users don’t need to understand changing prices. They only need to know that if they’re right, they’ll win something proportional to how many others were wrong. There’s an element of mystery to the exact payout (since it depends on total bets), but it’s generally intuitive: “fewer people on my side = bigger win if we’re right; more people on my side = smaller win.” Importantly, they won’t face the scenario of losing money on a correct prediction (except possibly only getting back ~1x if almost everyone predicted the same). - No need for users to trade or sell positions early – these markets are short (24h) and meant to be fire-and-forget. Removing the sell option simplifies both UI and contracts (your current UI doesn’t expose selling shares, and users are likely not looking to actively manage positions in a 24h micro-bet). - Gas usage might be lower per bet, since you’re just doing a simple transfer into the pool and updating totals (the AMM math is also not heavy, but pari-mutuel is as light as it gets).

**Cons:** Pari-mutuel doesn’t provide real-time odds feedback either – users won’t know their exact payout potential when betting (unless you show current pool totals and let them infer odds). But since you aren’t emphasizing real-time feedback, this is not a big downside. Also, one could argue it’s less “interactive” than an AMM (no price movement for users to exploit or trade on), but again, your target audience isn’t actively arbitraging odds – they’re swiping for fun. Another consideration: if users can see how many others have bet each side (and you might not show that until after, to avoid herding bias), popular opinion could sway decisions – but in a 24h resolution, this is minor.

**Recommendation:** For an MVP focused on simplicity and quick resolution, **lean towards a pari-mutuel betting system.** It aligns with your goal of _simpler backend logic and resolution_[\[6\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L9-L12)[\[9\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L198-L205), and it avoids the unintuitive outcomes that an un-disclosed AMM odds system might produce for casual users. Key implementation notes if you go this route:

- **Smart Contract Adjustments:** You can create a simplified market contract (or modify your existing one) to have:
- State: yesTotal and noTotal (track total stakes on each side), plus a mapping of user bets (e.g. mapping(address => uint256) yesBet and noBet for each market) so that each user’s contribution is known for payout. Since each user might only bet once per market via the swipe, this is straightforward – you’d just record the fixed bet amount they swiped with. (Your current contract tracks shares per user, which is similar but with that AMM conversion; you could repurpose it by treating “shares” equal to the stake for pari-mutuel, but it’s probably cleaner to write a new simple pool contract).
- Betting function: e.g. betYes(amount) and betNo(amount) (or a single function with a boolean). This would transferFrom the user’s USDC and add to yesTotal or noTotal and record yesBet\[msg.sender\] = amount (assuming one bet per user per market; if you allow multiple bets, you’d accumulate).
- **Fixed bet size:** Since you are fixing bet sizes at $1, $5, $10, you might even hardcode the allowed amounts or have the UI enforce it. The contract can simply expect one of those values (in 6 decimals for USDC). Fixed low amounts mean you’re not dealing with large variability – which is fine.
- **Resolution:** As with your current design, you (or a designated resolver/oracle) call a resolve(outcome) after 24 hours. The contract should require this only after end time and by authorized resolver (your existing resolveMarket function already has such checks[\[11\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L174-L183)). At resolution, you’d lock in the winning side.
- **Payout:** There are a couple ways to handle payouts:
  - **User-initiated claim:** After resolution, winners call a claim() function. The contract calculates their reward = their bet + (their bet / totalWinningSide) \* totalLosingSide. Then transfers that USDC to the user. This is trustless and lets users pull their winnings at their convenience. It requires storing individual bets and careful division math (to avoid precision issues). Given the small bet sizes, fixed-point math in 6 decimals should be okay.
  - **Automatic payout:** Alternatively, the contract could iterate over bettors and pay them out immediately at resolution. This is **not advisable on-chain** if there are many bettors, as looping over all bets can exceed gas or be exploitable. It’s better to let each user claim their share. You could also do a hybrid: small markets with few bettors could auto-distribute, but in general, a claim function is safer.
  - Note that in your current AMM model, you opted for a claim function (claimRewards()) that lets winners withdraw their USDC[\[12\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L195-L205). You can mimic that for pari-mutuel (just formula differs).
- **Example:** If you bet $5 on YES, and at resolution YES wins, your payout = $5 + ($5 / totalYesBets) \* totalNoBets. If NO wins, you get nothing, and your $5 stays in the contract to reward NO bettors. This way, the contract’s USDC balance will always exactly distribute to winners (no need for seeding liquidity since bets themselves fund the pool).
- **Comparison to current contract:** Your existing SimplePredictionMarket is more complex because it had to handle share calculations and allow buys/sells any time before resolution. If you choose pari-mutuel, you essentially **strip out the dynamic pricing and trading features**. This simplification aligns with your note that _real-time market price feedback isn't important for the user_, and that you want the **creator (you) to manually resolve after 24h**[\[13\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/MarketFactory.sol#L54-L62)[\[11\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L174-L183). The result is a straightforward “place bet, wait 24h, get result” flow, which suits the casual vibe of Tomo.
- **One outcome = double-or-nothing feel:** Pari-mutuel markets can be framed to the user as basically “win or lose your fixed stake” with some variability in how much you win if you’re right. If the sides are balanced, a correct bet roughly doubles your money (minus any small cut if you plan to take a fee). If the sides are uneven, a correct bet could do slightly less or much more than double. Users will experience a _clear win_ vs _loss_ outcome, which is satisfying in a gameified app. In contrast, the AMM share model can feel more like incremental trading profits/losses which is less tangible to an average user.

**Conclusion on model:** For the MVP, I recommend implementing the **pari-mutuel style** for simplicity and user clarity. You can always evolve back to an AMM model later if you want to introduce advanced features like real-time odds, trading, or liquidity provision. But starting simple will make development faster and the UX more intuitive. Since you already have a working codebase, you might adapt some structures (e.g. your MarketFactory can still deploy each market contract; just the market contract code changes to a simpler version). And because markets resolve quickly (24h), the lack of continuous trading is not a drawback at all – it might even encourage users to come back daily to see outcomes and new cards.

# PHASE 3: SMART CONTRACTS & GASLESS INTEGRATION
## Smart Contracts & Gasless Integration on Base

Regardless of the betting mechanism, you need to ensure the contracts integrate with your **Account Abstraction (ERC-4337) and Paymaster** setup for a gasless UX. You’ve already deployed on Base Sepolia and configured Coinbase’s Developer Paymaster with your contract allowlist[\[14\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/DEPLOYED_CONTRACTS.md#L90-L98). A few points to keep in mind:

- **Paymaster allowlist:** If you introduce new market contract code (for pari-mutuel) or redeploy factory, update the paymaster’s allowed addresses and methods. Your deployment notes show you already adding the MarketFactory and Demo Market to the allowlist[\[14\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/DEPLOYED_CONTRACTS.md#L90-L98). Make sure the new Factory/Market addresses on Base Mainnet are also allowed so users’ transactions are sponsored.
- **Batching via AA:** The batching mechanism we discussed ties into how the user’s wallet (likely a smart contract wallet via OnchainKit/MiniKit) can bundle multiple calls. In your hack project, you used Coinbase’s **OnchainKit** which provides a &lt;Transaction&gt; component to batch multiple calls and send one user operation[\[15\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L513-L521). This means when a batch triggers, you create an array of calls to different market contracts (each call being a bet on a market) and submit them together. Continue to leverage this – it’s working well per your demo. Whether it’s buyShares() calls or betYes()/betNo() calls, the concept is the same. Ensure your paymaster is okay with multiple internal calls; since you’ve tested “20 predictions in one batch” on Sepolia, it seems to handle it.
- **EntryPoint and reentrancy:** Your current contracts have guards for the EntryPoint (ERC-4337) reentrancy[\[16\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/MarketFactory.sol#L19-L27)[\[17\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/MarketFactory.sol#L156-L164). You should include similar protections in new contracts, or at least be mindful of reentrancy if your contract callbacks (though simple pool betting is less prone to reentrancy issues than an AMM perhaps). The Base AA flow is still evolving, but following best practices as you did (using the \_entryPointReentrancyStatus pattern) is smart.
- **USDC token integration:** You’ve chosen USDC as the currency for bets (with 6 decimals). Continue using USDC on Base for real-money feel (micropredictions in dollars). This requires users to have some USDC, but since transactions are gasless, they don’t need ETH for gas. Keep in mind for Mainnet you’ll need a source of USDC liquidity for users (perhaps fiat onramp or prompts to bridge USDC to Base). Technically, the contracts just need the USDC contract address (which you have for testnet[\[18\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/DEPLOYED_CONTRACTS.md#L9-L17) and will have for mainnet). No changes needed there.
- **Fixed bet sizing in UI vs. contract:** You mentioned $1 default with option for $5 or $10. The simplest approach is to handle this on the UI side – let the user toggle their bet amount, and when swiping, include that amount in the batch call. Your defaultBetAmount in the user store is already set to 1 by default[\[19\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L104-L112), and used when queuing a prediction[\[20\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L154-L162). So just ensure the contract can accept those different amounts. (If you want to enforce only those values, you could hardcode a require check on-chain, but it’s probably unnecessary if the UI controls it and it’s all your own front-end.)
- **Transaction confirmation UX:** Because you cover gas, the user will never see a gas fee. However, if using a smart wallet, they might need to sign the transaction or authorize it. OnchainKit likely handles this via the TransactionButton (which presumably triggers the signing). In testing, see if you can streamline this – perhaps a one-time login signature and then auto-send batches without user approving each time (session keys, etc., if supported). The current flow shows a modal asking to confirm predictions[\[5\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L519-L527), which might be acceptable since it’s a simple tap and not a full wallet confirmation dialog. It’s a trade-off: fully automatic might be ideal for flow, but a confirm step can prevent mistakes. Given your target is to **“not let users think”**, pushing toward auto-send (no extra confirmation per batch) could be desirable, as long as security is maintained (maybe limit daily spend via the paymaster or wallet to the bet amounts).
- **Performance:** Batching 20 calls in one UserOperation is fine, but test with larger batches if you plan to increase the limit. Also consider concurrent usage – if many users are swiping simultaneously, your backend (Supabase + any off-chain processing) and the bundler need to handle the load. The **Supabase** is logging each prediction and updating user positions in real-time[\[21\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L311-L320)[\[22\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L337-L346). That’s great for the leaderboard and social features, but keep an eye on race conditions or slightly delayed writes. In particular, your code checks for duplicate prediction entries and updates positions after a batch is confirmed[\[23\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L321-L330)[\[24\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L339-L348) – ensure this logic still makes sense if you switch to pari-mutuel (you might not deal with “shares_received” in the same way; perhaps just store the amount as both stake and “shares” for simplicity, since in pari-mutuel one could consider the “share” equal to the stake amount for logging purposes).

In summary, the technical integration on Base with gasless transactions is largely in place. Just adjust the contract logic for the chosen betting model, and update the paymaster config if needed. The goal is that from the user’s perspective, **swiping = betting without friction** – thanks to your behind-the-scenes batching and sponsored transactions.

# PHASE 4: SOCIAL FEATURES
## Social Features: Leaderboards, Streaks, and Sharing

To leverage the “social + micro prediction” aspect of Tomo, implementing community features is key. You’ve identified **leaderboards, streaks, and sharing** as essential. Here’s how to incorporate them effectively:

- **Leaderboards:** A live leaderboard will tap into competitive instincts. You already have a **“Live leaderboard with user rankings”** in your feature list[\[25\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/README.md#L72-L76), and your database schema tracks stats like win_rate, total_predictions, and profit_loss for users (in an InfluencerProfile or user profile table)[\[26\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/lib/supabase.ts#L41-L50). To build the leaderboard:
- Decide the ranking criteria: It could be **total winnings/profit**, **number of correct predictions**, **win rate** (percentage correct), or even **longest active streak**. For a fun app, **streaks** or total correct predictions might be more engaging than raw profit in dollars, given bets are tiny. Users will brag about how many they got right in a row or their overall accuracy.
- Update stats after each market resolution: Since markets resolve every 24h, you’ll likely have a daily influx of results. You can update each user’s record: increment total_predictions, increment correct_predictions if they won, update win_rate = correct/total, and update profit_loss (they win +X or lose their stake). This can be done off-chain in Supabase when you process resolution outcomes. Your current setup might not automatically handle resolution outcomes in the app yet – you’ll need a script or process to mark markets as resolved in the DB and then update user stats accordingly.
- Display: The leaderboard can reset periodically (weekly, monthly) or have categories (e.g. “All-time top predictors” vs “This week’s streak leaders”). Given the short-term focus, a weekly reset could keep competition fresh. Also consider **categories** – someone might specialize in sports predictions vs crypto. You could eventually have category-specific leaderboards, but for MVP a global board is fine.
- Real-time updates: Using Supabase real-time subscriptions, you can push leaderboard changes as they happen. This gives engaged users instant feedback – e.g. “You moved up to #3!” after a win. It appears you already considered real-time updates via Supabase for positions[\[25\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/README.md#L72-L76), so extending that to leaderboard entries is natural.
- **Streaks:** Highlighting a user’s consecutive correct picks can drive addiction, as people strive not to break their streak:
- Track each user’s current streak (and maybe their best streak). This isn’t explicitly in your schema, but you can derive it. For example, each time a market resolves, check if the user was correct. If yes, increment their streak count; if no, reset it to 0. Also, if streak > best_streak, update that.
- Display the streak prominently: e.g. on the user’s profile (“🔥 7 in a row correct!”) and perhaps on the leaderboard (“Streak: 7” next to their name).
- You might even have **streak-based rewards** or badges (though that can be a later gamification layer). For now, simply showing the streak is motivating. Users might also share screenshots of their streaks, indirectly promoting the app.
- **Sharing Markets & Social Trading:** You noted that sharing cards (markets) created by users is important for community growth. This has a few facets:
- **User-Created Markets:** Eventually, allowing users to create their own prediction questions will boost engagement (people love seeing others bet on their question). Your platform already supports user-created markets via the MarketFactory[\[27\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/README.md#L68-L76). For MVP, you (the team) might create most markets, but keep the door open for users to propose or create markets in-app. When they do, give them credit (e.g. display the creator’s handle on the card).
- **Sharing links:** Implement a share button on each market card that generates a URL or deep link. This lets a user share a prediction question with friends on Twitter, Telegram, etc. For example, “I just bet YES on ‘Will BTC hit $30k tomorrow?’ on Tomo – what do you think?” with a link. Since Base is an L2, you can even link to a read-only view of the market on a web frontend. **Deep linking** could directly open the app to that question if the recipient has Tomo installed.
- **Internal sharing / Challenges:** You might introduce a feature where users can **challenge friends** on a market. For instance, after betting, a user can ping a friend (if you have a friend/follow system via those follower_count fields[\[28\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/lib/supabase.ts#L41-L48)) to try the same question. This taps into social competition. While possibly beyond MVP, it’s aligned with “social prediction.”
- **Influencer integration:** Your schema shows fields for linking to Farcaster and Twitter[\[29\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/lib/supabase.ts#L51-L58). This suggests you might integrate with those socials – e.g., pulling in an influencer’s profile or allowing login via those platforms. In the interim, consider running campaigns where known crypto or sports personalities create markets on Tomo and encourage their followers to vote. Because Tomo is micro-stakes, it’s not as intimidating as betting – it can be positioned as a fun interactive poll with a token reward for being right. That’s highly shareable content.
- **Community content around results:** After a market resolves, make it share-worthy. E.g., “✅ Market Resolved: 72% bet YES and won!” – a summary graphic or text that users can share. This not only recognizes winners (and perhaps tags the market creator) but also demonstrates the activity in the app. If you have a **feed or community page**, posting resolved results and tagging top winners could spur more engagement.
- **Scaling with social features:** As you said, _without leaderboards and sharing, it’d be impossible to scale_. These features drive viral growth and retention:
- Leaderboard gives people a reason to keep coming (even if the money at stake is tiny, the _status_ is the reward).
- Streaks encourage daily play (nobody wants to break a streak – similar to how Duolingo or other apps keep people hooked).
- Sharing brings new users in (each shared market is an invitation for others to participate, potentially converting passive followers into new players on Tomo).

Make sure to monitor how these social features affect behavior. For example, if a leaderboard is purely by profit, whales or lucky long-shot bettors might dominate – which could discourage others. A streak or accuracy-based board might be more inclusive for casual players. You might even maintain multiple leaderboards (e.g. one for streaks, one for total wins, etc.) to give players different goals to shoot for.

Technically, implementing these likely means extending your Supabase schema and writing some backend jobs: - When a market is resolved (you have a server or admin process calling the contract’s resolve and updating the DB), calculate outcomes for each user who bet. You can store the result in user_predictions table (maybe a boolean flag if their prediction was correct) and then update the aggregate stats in InfluencerProfile or a similar user stats table. - Supabase can trigger functions or webhooks on inserts/updates, so you might set up a stored procedure or simply use your app server to do the computation. - The **frontend** can subscribe to changes or fetch updated rankings periodically. You already have a Leaderboard.tsx component listed[\[30\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/README.md#L88-L91); ensure it’s wired up to the right data.

## Conclusion & Next Steps

**Tomo’s** appeal will lie in its effortless UX and social hooks. By batching transactions (e.g. after ~20 swipes or short idle periods) with a gasless AA wallet, users get a **frictionless “swipe to predict” experience**[\[1\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/README.md#L63-L67)[\[3\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/app/components/PredictionMarket.tsx#L226-L234). Choosing the **pari-mutuel betting system** for now will keep the backend logic simple and outcomes intuitive – every correct swipe yields a win share of the pool, with no complex price mechanics. This aligns with resolving markets in 24 hours and not worrying about live odds[\[6\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L9-L12)[\[9\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/src/SimplePredictionMarket.sol#L198-L205).

Meanwhile, layering in the **community features** – live leaderboards, streak tracking, and easy sharing – will turn predictions into a social game rather than solitary gambling. Users will come back not just to win a few dollars, but to compete with friends, maintain bragging rights on the leaderboard, and contribute questions to the community.

With your Base Sepolia tests already successful (using real USDC and Coinbase Paymaster)[\[18\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/DEPLOYED_CONTRACTS.md#L9-L17)[\[14\]](https://github.com/avdheshcharjan/SEER/blob/2e0d32b0606c4af8dae74b1fdabd632bf83fc0b5/contracts/DEPLOYED_CONTRACTS.md#L90-L98), you’re on a solid path. The remaining steps are to adjust the contracts if moving to pari-mutuel, polish the batching logic timing based on testing (e.g. try that 60-second window vs. shorter, see what keeps engagement highest), and implement the stat tracking for the social features. Once that’s done, deploy to Base mainnet and let users start swiping. Given the “brainrot” addictive design, Tomo could very well become the TikTok of prediction markets – quick, fun, and rewarding in more ways than one.

Good luck, and happy building! 🚀
